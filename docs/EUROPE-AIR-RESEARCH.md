# Flights over Europe — research note

**13 September 2026 · Potential future study · Thesis unresolved**

[Study index](README.md) · [Series goals](VISION.md) · [Catalogue admission criteria](CATALOGUE.md) · [Recorded air pipeline](AIR-DATA.md) · [Live airport service](LIVE-AIRPORTS.md)

A recorded study of aircraft moving across Europe appears technically plausible with the existing Motion Studies air pipeline. Continental acquisition, compilation and browser performance have not been measured. The more consequential open question is what the work would reveal: which relationship becomes perceptible through motion, what makes that relationship particular to Europe, and what the viewer understands after watching it.

This note records evidence and proposes experiments for a future study. It does not select an edition identity, assign a catalogue number or commit the programme to implementation.

## Personal geography and motivation

On 13 September 2026, the author identified an emerging pattern in the choice of subjects: connection and emotional attachment draw attention toward Europe and Britain, with substantially less interest in Asia or the Americas. This is an emerging artistic orientation, not a fixed geographic exclusion or a decision about existing candidate studies.

That attachment supplies a meaningful answer to why this region merits sustained attention. Geographic breadth and available data need not determine the subjects of the series. Familiar places can support a more attentive work through the choice of viewpoint, journeys, scale and moments worth dwelling on.

The remaining thesis question is what motion allows the author and viewer to discover about those places. A phenomenon need not be exclusive to Europe to support a distinctly situated composition. Personal motivation explains the choice of subject; observations support what the work says about it.

One possible direction to explore is the relationship between Britain and the wider European air network: familiar geography seen through departures, crossings and arrivals. This is a new composition prompt, not an adopted thesis or an assumption about the author's own journeys. A narrower frame may carry more meaning than comprehensive continental coverage.

## The research question

**What can a day of flight make visible about Europe that a static route map cannot?**

“Flights over Europe” supplies a subject and a geographic extent. An authored study still needs a claim. The interesting possibility is a changing relationship between cities, time and airspace: places becoming connected in pulses; paths accumulating into temporary structures; movements entering and leaving a frame whose boundaries are only ours.

Beauty should help expose that relationship. Trails might reveal recurrence, fading might distinguish a passing wave from a permanent network, and a change of viewpoint might separate movements that overlap in plan. Those choices need an evidential purpose. A dense field of luminous aircraft could be compelling, but density alone would not answer the research question.

The series' admission test asks for a sentence that depends on its place and a signature composition. At continental scale, the equivalent is to explain why these European cities, distances, connections or boundaries matter. Each hypothesis below remains open to being narrowed, combined or rejected.

## Candidate theses and their visual tests

### 1. Cities connected in time

**Hypothesis:** The observed network between European cities repeatedly forms and disperses through departure and arrival waves. Its structure changes over the day.

**What could be beautiful:** A quiet map gathers movements around several cities, releases them into crossing streams, then concentrates them elsewhere. The viewer can follow a wave across the same continuous clock. Arrivals and departures remain distinguishable without turning every airport into a dashboard.

**What it could demonstrate:** Temporal concentration and different rhythms at selected airports; how simultaneous movements assemble a network that a static route diagram flattens into permanent connections.

**First test:** Compare observed arrival/departure event counts in fixed time bins at several well-covered airports. Preview two short windows with different activity patterns, alongside a small time plot. Establish whether the proposed waves are visible in the observations before designing a sequence around them.

**What would weaken it:** The apparent rhythm comes mainly from receiver availability, clipping or trail settings; the composition reveals little about the chosen places beyond a generic pattern. Passenger connections, deliberate airline coordination and the reasons for a wave require evidence beyond nearby aircraft and coincident timestamps.

This is a promising first composition test because the existing clock and airport-selection behaviour already support it. That practical advantage does not establish it as the final thesis.

### 2. An architecture that movement draws

**Hypothesis:** Repeated observed paths reveal a structured airspace above Europe, with corridors, convergence and separation that are difficult to perceive one aircraft at a time.

**What could be beautiful:** Short-lived traces gradually make a spatial structure legible. A controlled transition from plan to an oblique view reveals vertical differences within selected crossings, then returns to the continental field.

**What it could demonstrate:** Where sampled trajectories recur and how observed traffic occupies horizontal and vertical space during a defined interval.

**First test:** Find a well-observed corridor and a crossing region. Compare instantaneous aircraft, short trails and a clearly labelled accumulation over the same interval. Test whether altitude adds understanding at this scale or simply exaggerates the scene.

**What would weaken it:** The pattern only appears under excessive accumulation or vertical distortion. Observed paths do not establish published airways, assigned flight levels, air-traffic-control intent or safe separation. Explaining those would require additional sources and appropriate altitude interpretation.

### 3. Europe as a passage

**Hypothesis:** The airspace over Europe is shaped by traffic whose journeys extend beyond it; a continental boundary conceals part of the system that produces the visible motion.

**What could be beautiful:** Streams arrive from outside the frame, cross or settle into European airports, and later depart beyond the horizon. Selecting a well-observed journey briefly expands the frame, making the relationship between a regional view and a longer journey tangible.

**What it could demonstrate:** The coexistence of observed internal journeys, inbound/outbound movements and overflights, where endpoint evidence supports that distinction.

**First test:** Define one geographic frame explicitly and inspect selected full traces beyond it. Measure how many segments have credible endpoint evidence before attempting category totals. A track crossing an edge only demonstrates a boundary crossing; it does not identify its origin or destination.

**What would weaken it:** Full journeys are too incomplete to classify, or the visual argument depends on invented destination arcs. This candidate could develop a more specific geographic argument, but needs stronger route evidence than a replay of clipped positions.

### 4. Several clocks in one sky

**Hypothesis:** A common UTC view reveals different local daily rhythms across European airports and routes.

**What could be beautiful:** Activity rises and recedes unevenly across a fixed map. Selected local clocks make the differences understandable; daylight could supply a restrained, calculated reference if it materially helps the reading.

**What it could demonstrate:** How observed activity relates to local civil time on the selected dates, and whether meaningful differences survive comparison on one shared timeline.

**First test:** Compare activity profiles in UTC and local time at a small set of airports. Include another comparable date before describing a recurring pattern. Add daylight only if it reveals something beyond a familiar animated terminator.

**What would weaken it:** The picture suggests a simple east-to-west awakening that the observations do not support. Curfews, demand, scheduling decisions and causal effects of daylight need separate evidence.

## Questions that should decide the work

- What should a viewer be able to say after a minute that they could not say from the opening still?
- Which moment or transformation carries that discovery? Does it remain legible on a phone?
- Why Europe? Could the same sentence and composition be transferred unchanged to another region?
- Is the subject airport rhythm, the structure of airspace, or the relationship between Europe and journeys beyond it? Which should remain secondary?
- Does the thesis need individual aircraft, accumulated paths, airport events, or a deliberate transition between those representations?
- Is one dated day sufficient for the claim, or is comparison across days essential?

Keep naming open until a composition provides an answer. “Flights over Europe” is a research label.

### Initial title contributions

**LUFTRAUM** and **ALOFT** were suggested by the author on 13 September 2026 as early possibilities, with reservations about their distinctiveness and an expectation that more candidates will follow. Neither is selected or preferred.

- **LUFTRAUM** suggests airspace as the subject: its extent, structure and occupation by movement. It could suit the hypothesis about an architecture drawn by trajectories. The name already identifies the optional Swiss air study in [Gleislicht](GLEISLICHT.md), so a future continental use would need a deliberate relationship to that existing name.
- **ALOFT** suggests the experience of being airborne: height, suspension and movement above the ground. It could support a more atmospheric composition, though it does not yet imply a particular European relationship or visual argument.

These are editorial associations to explore, rather than settled theses. Keep the list open and revisit it alongside the first compositions.

## The role of AeroDataBox

AeroDataBox adds schedule and operational context. The present adapter requests airport boards with location disabled and does not retain aircraft positions or historical flight archives. Access to this integration therefore does not itself supply a continental trajectory feed. Recorded motion would continue to use ADSB.lol; any provider enrichment would need a dated match with explicit unknown or ambiguous cases. See the [current adapter](../services/airports/provider.ts) and [service design](LIVE-AIRPORTS.md).

The provider describes coverage as varying by airport and region, with distinct schedule, live-status and ADS-B layers. Its country percentages are not measures of continuous trajectory completeness. Current airport feed status should be checked when choosing a proof. [AeroDataBox coverage](https://aerodatabox.com/data-coverage/).

For scale context, the current board integration budgets two units per refresh. At five-minute intervals, 20 continuously requested airports would use `20 × 288 × 2 × 31 = 357,120` units over 31 days, before retries or other account use. Demand-driven fetching can reduce this; it is an illustrative upper routine rate, not a proposed service configuration. Our deployed service currently caps usage at 1,000 units per UTC day and 30,000 per calendar month. [Local budget policy](LIVE-AIRPORTS.md#cost-and-fallback).

On 13 September 2026, the published Direct plans list Starter at $19/month for 40,000 units with seven-day retention, and Growth at $99/month for 400,000 units with retention while subscribed. A lasting study containing provider data needs its storage and publication model checked against the applicable terms. Recheck these conditions when revisiting the proposal. ## A bounded research sequence

1. **Choose a question and frame.** Select one primary hypothesis and one alternative. Define the geographic extent and whether the object of study is traffic over the region or journeys connecting its airports. Record why that boundary helps the question.
2. **Audit a small source window.** Start with a one- or two-hour interval, potentially on 4 September 2026 to align with existing editions, subject to archive availability and coverage. Retain source hashes, filtering rules, gaps and endpoint uncertainty. Audit coverage before choosing the most attractive moment.
3. **Make two comparable compositions.** Use the same observations and clock to test two readings, such as city pulses and recurring corridors. Vary one meaningful visual choice at a time. Neither version needs a finished application.
4. **Review the visual claim.** Ask viewers what relationship they noticed and where the image showed it. Decide whether the proposed thesis was visible, whether it depended on Europe, and whether a written explanation supplied more than the image demonstrated.
5. **Measure technical limits.** Record download/decoded size, peak compiler and browser memory, time to first motion, frame-time distribution, seeking and selection on a named phone/browser. Set an opening budget from those results. Smaller chunks, spatial loading and simplified distant views are possible responses, not yet proven requirements.
6. **Broaden only to test a surviving claim.** A full day tests temporal development; another comparable day tests recurrence. Additional seasons or disruption days need their own question and source review.

Any downsampling experiment must preserve the distinction between missing observations and intentional simplification. The current player refuses interpolation across gaps longer than 45 seconds; reducing sample frequency without coordinating that contract can make valid tracks disappear. Longer interpolation across real gaps would manufacture apparent continuity.

## Decision at resumption

Advance to an edition brief when a bounded composition reveals a defensible relationship, the evidence supports its interpretation, and measured delivery fits the series' phone-first goals. Narrow the geography if the strongest argument belongs to one corridor or group of airports. Retain the experiment as technical research if it proves scale without establishing an authored work.

The present conclusion is **plausible engineering, unresolved thesis**. The next useful evidence is a small comparison of visual arguments and source quality. A full European ingest can follow if that comparison gives the study a reason to exist.
