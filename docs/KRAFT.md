**1 November 2026 — study brief and first source proof.** KRAFT is an unnumbered European electricity study. The title and subtitle are the working identity; the first six-country data probe is complete. The independent [private KRAFT scaffold](https://github.com/emmettl/kraft) now opens the verified first recorded day. The visual grammar and public release remain open.

## The proposition

**A recorded day of European electricity: where power is generated, how demand changes, and what crosses the borders between them.**

As daylight moves across Europe, its places produce and consume electricity in different rhythms. KRAFT attends to their changing relationship: generation rising or falling, demand changing, a border exchange strengthening or reversing. The work asks how the balance between connected places changes across a shared day.

England explored power as a second witness beside transport. Here electricity carries the work. The continental scale introduces the exchanges between places as a central subject, while preserving a route from the whole to one reported interval.

The opening hypothesis is that changing generation, demand and exchanges can form a legible composition without relying on a dense drawing of transmission infrastructure. A day can test that proposition; it cannot establish a typical European rhythm or explain why operators made particular decisions.

## The first experience

Open on a restrained continental map and one dated clock. Daylight moves across the surface. Each included reporting area holds its generation mix and demand; directional bands between areas change width with recorded physical exchange. Country totals occupy their reporting areas without pretending to locate generation or consumption within them. Uncovered places remain visible as geography, with coverage distinct from zero.

The defining gesture is to **hold a border, follow its changing exchange, then return to the continent**. Selecting a band reveals its two areas, direction, power, source interval and daily history. The clock and selection survive the descent. At a reversal, the band passes through zero before changing direction. Animated marks indicate the sign and magnitude of an aggregate; their speed does not represent electricity's travel time.

Selecting an area reveals generation by type, load and exchanges on aligned time axes. Storage generation and charging remain separate. Daylight supplies geographic context; weather can join later with its own evidence and model labels. Changes occurring together are available for inspection without implying a causal explanation.

## Visual direction

**A place holds its balance while everything passing through it changes.** The visual surprise comes from discovering substantial, simultaneous exchanges behind a small net total. The [Swiss passage](KRAFT-SWITZERLAND.md) gives this proposition a concrete first setting: persistent French imports and Italian exports alongside a German exchange that changes direction.

**The visual grammar remains to be found.** A junction that gradually becomes a weave is one experiment. Reversal, simultaneous opposing exchanges, balance and accumulation may suggest other forms. Power could shape a changing edge while accumulated energy gives the day a lasting form. Each treatment should make its quantities and transformations intelligible without prescribing its appearance in advance.

Wind and light introduce a possible interplay of elements: atmospheric movement, the passage of daylight, generation and electrical exchange. Their different rhythms may become the substance of the composition. Meteorological wind, wind-generated electricity, astronomical daylight and solar-generated electricity are distinct quantities and phenomena. Their visual relationships should preserve those distinctions. A weather field would need its own dated source and evidence treatment; daylight alone does not establish solar output.

It remains open whether KRAFT becomes primarily spatial, primarily abstract, or allows a transition between the two. Geography can give a relationship its place; abstraction can let magnitude, direction, duration and balance determine the form more freely. A transition may let a viewer carry an understanding between these experiences. It earns its place if the same relationship remains recognisable through the change, with time and selection preserved.

The concepts below are studies to compare using the same evidence. An accumulated composition could also work as a still image or print, with its date, scale and coverage intact. Neither the map nor the weave is yet the required final form.

### A country becomes a junction

Broad, translucent bands enter and leave a selected reporting area. Their widths change with recorded power. The geography stays quiet enough that a country can appear almost still while considerable exchange takes place around it. Selecting Germany's connection to Switzerland holds that band in view as its direction changes; the French and Italian exchanges continue faintly at the edges.

Each band ends at the reporting area. Separate entrances and exits preserve the simultaneous relationships without drawing an unsupported continuous journey from France through Switzerland to Italy. A net-balance reading remains available beside them, making the difference between net and separate exchanges visible.

### The day leaves a woven trace

Each selected border unfolds into a ribbon along a shared time axis. Width records magnitude; the side of a fine centreline records direction relative to the selected area. A reversal moves the ribbon to the other side, making a fold in the day's trace. Consistent scales allow the persistent French and Italian exchanges to be read beside the changing German relationship.

The weave is an authored arrangement of independent border histories. Crossings in the layout do not mean electrical connections or transfers between ribbons. Time, direction and magnitude must remain recoverable from the image. Missing intervals break the trace; they do not narrow it to zero.

The moving map can gradually unfold into this accumulated view. Preserve the selected border, clock and colour through the transformation so the viewer recognises the same evidence in its new form. Scrubbing the weave returns to the corresponding geographic state. Test the transformation first on Switzerland's four borders before expanding to a continental composition.

### The map opens into an instrument

As attention descends, the selected area opens into aligned histories of generation, demand and each exchange. Faint geographic connections remain while temporal relationships become easier to inspect. Returning to the map keeps the selected relationship illuminated. The transformation is part of understanding the work: a pattern noticed in the histories changes how the geography is seen.

### Daylight crosses several electrical days

Astronomical daylight moves continuously across Europe while reported generation changes at native source intervals. Solar production rises unevenly beneath it; hydro, wind and demand retain their own rhythms. Daylight supplies a common geographic clock without becoming an explanation for every change. Keep its brightness restrained and distinguish smooth astronomical motion from the interval evidence beneath it.

### Stored water introduces a slower time

A later Swiss treatment could place the day's changing exchanges beside the slow seasonal record of stored water. A weekly regional reservoir trace would remain almost still during a day's replay, giving the faster movement another duration against which to be felt. It belongs on a separate, explicit time scale. It cannot animate individual lakes emptying or establish pumping activity during the day.

### Motion and visual language

Keep motion slow enough to notice a band becoming thinner, holding at a small value and changing direction. Width carries power, direction carries sign, and a small stable palette carries relationship identity. Reserve brightness for attention rather than adding a second unexplained quantitative scale.

At a sign change between reported intervals, the transition through zero is a visual convention. Keep the native interval values inspectable and avoid implying that zero or the exact reversal time was measured. Gaps remain visibly different from zero. Texture and moving marks may make direction legible, but do not represent individual electrons or electrical propagation speed.

The first visual experiments should compare spatial and abstract treatments of the same frozen Swiss intervals, including a junction, a weave and forms derived from reversal, balance or accumulation. Explore wind and light where dated evidence supports their inclusion. Test a transition between representations as its own proposition: does it deepen perception of the relationship, and can the viewer retain time, identity and a route to the evidence? Review what each treatment reveals through motion and what remains legible in a still. Let these experiments determine the eventual visual grammar.

## Three scales of attention

| Scale | Experience | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continent | Changing generation and exchanges across a common clock | Included areas, borders, measures, units and coverage |
| Relationship | A selected area's neighbours, or a stated group of borders | Explicit membership and contributing series, counted once |
| Interval | One border or production-type value at a dated time | Provider response, native interval, transformations, retrieval and licence |

The first descent ends at an area or border measurement. Station-level output and cited site locations could deepen selected places later. A coloured country cannot be unfolded into individual generators unless a separate source actually supplies them.

## First recorded day

Use **16 September 2026, 00:00–24:00 UTC**, as the first technical candidate. The [source audit](KRAFT-SOURCES.md) verified generation/load and physical-flow responses for the Netherlands, Germany, France, Switzerland, Austria and Italy. This date is an inspectable starting point, not a claim of representativeness or an editorially final day.

The opening viewing area runs from the North Sea through France and Germany to the Alps and Italy. Italy's first acquired generation series is national, so the work must label it Italy even if the camera favours the north. External exchanges remain identifiable at the edge; a cropped map is an open system.

The first composition should test a whole-day passage, one changing border relationship and one quieter interval. Germany–Switzerland and Germany–Netherlands both reverse direction in the acquired day, providing concrete candidates for close attention. Missing Italian storage values remain gaps. Swiss generation/load stays visibly hourly beside quarter-hourly flows.

## Visual and evidential commitments

- Band width has a stated power scale shared across borders. Any nonlinear overview treatment is disclosed; selection gives the original quantity.
- Physical exchanges and commercial trades keep separate identities. A trade series embedded in a generation response never becomes measured flow.
- Country-to-country bands are schematic connections. They do not claim a particular cable, transmission route, supplying station or destination city.
- Missing values stay missing. Smooth visual transitions never create additional observations or turn hourly evidence into quarter-hourly measurement.
- Generation, load, charging and exchanges retain their definitions. An unexplained balance residual stays visible rather than being forced to zero.
- Source and licence information travel with the compiled day and the evidence view.

## First proof and decision gates

The [Swiss passage investigation](KRAFT-SWITZERLAND.md) develops the first close view: simultaneous French imports and Italian exports, a reversing German exchange, national hydro categories and a possible descent into modelled district solar production. Its source discrepancies and missing pumping-consumption evidence define the current limits.

The [extended survey](KRAFT-SURVEY.md) supplies a tested route to historical wind and radiation and a local observed weather counterpoint. Quarter-hourly Swiss regional records can deepen spatial studies subject to rights and clock interpretation. The comparison days include a German exchange with no reversal; experiments should allow both persistent and changing relationships. Keep the Swissgrid and Energy-Charts accounts separate until the load and French-border discrepancies are explained.

Build one offline replay from frozen inputs, with no provider requests from the browser. Use the existing daylight and timeline primitives where their meanings fit. Keep acquisition in the recorder and the composition in the eventual KRAFT edition; European area/edge semantics should be designed before extending England's per-unit power-day contract.

The proof succeeds if a viewer can recognise a changing relationship, select a border and reach its original interval without losing time or place. It weakens if movement is mainly decorative, the geography implies unsupported routes, or differences in source coverage dominate the picture.

Before publication, complete the source and rights gates in the audit, reconcile every displayed value to frozen input, review the boundary exchanges and choose the day on both coverage and visual evidence. Catalogue numbering follows the series admission process. No live collection or hosting is required for this proof.
